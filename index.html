About Me

Academic Background

I am a senior-year Physics student at Birla Institute of Technology, Mesra, pursuing an Integrated M.Sc. in Physics with a minor in Computer Science. My interests lie primarily in astronomy and cosmology — particularly dark matter, gravitational waves, astroparticle physics, dark energy, modified gravity, galaxies, stellar evolution, and the large-scale structure of the universe.

I enjoy exploring these problems through observational astronomy, computational modeling, statistical techniques, and theoretical physics, with a growing interest in the intersection between data-driven astrophysics and numerical cosmology.

Research Direction

My approach to physics is guided by an interdisciplinary perspective that spans observation, computation, statistical inference, and theoretical modeling. Rather than restricting myself to a single methodological framework, I aim to develop fluency across multiple approaches to better understand complex physical systems.

I view modern astrophysics and cosmology as deeply interconnected fields where observational data, numerical simulations, and analytical theory complement one another. Given the increasing availability of large astronomical datasets and computational resources, I believe developing experience across multiple approaches is valuable at this stage of training before specializing more deeply during graduate research.

My research journey reflects this progression. I began with introductory data-analysis-oriented projects that helped me build computational intuition. I then moved toward theoretical and numerical work in gravitational physics through a project on quasi-normal modes of wormholes, where I gained exposure to perturbation methods, numerical techniques, and gravitational wave physics.

Later, I worked with real observational datasets from UVIT/AstroSat and Gaia, gaining experience in multiwavelength astronomy, catalog cross-matching, stellar population analysis, and astrophysical data interpretation. I then extended this into dark matter physics, deriving constraints on dark matter–baryon scattering from the thermal properties of the Milky Way's circumgalactic medium, and most recently into gravitational-wave cosmology, building a joint Bayesian inference pipeline for dark-siren $H_0$ measurements. Currently, I am exploring computational and statistical approaches relevant to modern astrophysics and cosmology.

Long-Term Vision

In the long term, I hope to pursue a Ph.D. in computational cosmology or astrophysics, ideally combining theoretical physics, numerical simulations, observational data analysis, and statistical methods.

Research Intern — Dark Matter–Baryon Scattering Constraints from the Milky Way Circumgalactic Medium (CGM)

Derived upper limits on the dark matter–baryon scattering cross section using the thermal properties of the Milky Way's circumgalactic medium (CGM). Investigated four velocity-dependent interaction models (n = 2, 0, −2, −4) and compared the resulting CGM constraints with existing CMB limits.

Indian Institute of Astrophysics

Research Intern — Disentangling Stellar Populations in the Western Halo of the SMC Using UVIT and Gaia DR3

Investigated stellar populations in the western outskirts of the Small Magellanic Cloud as the first author and primary investigator, leading the full research workflow from data acquisition to analysis and interpretation. The study combined ultraviolet observations from UVIT/AstroSat FUV & NUV with optical photometry and astrometric data from Gaia to construct a multiwavelength view of the region.

I developed and executed the complete data analysis pipeline, including cross-matching multi-catalog datasets, source cleaning and filtering, and construction of stellar population diagnostics such as color–magnitude diagrams and spatial distribution maps. Applied advanced analysis techniques such as vector point diagrams, Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM), and kernel density estimation (KDE) to study stellar kinematics and separate Milky Way foreground stars (including 47 Tucanae) from SMC members, followed by isochrone fitting on UV/optical color–magnitude and color–color diagrams.

The analysis focused on identifying multiple stellar populations in this relatively underexplored outer region of the Small Magellanic Cloud and investigating possible tidal interactions. The results suggest population gradients and dynamical influence in the western outskirts, contributing to the understanding of the galaxy’s outer structure and evolutionary history.

Supervisors: Prof. Annapurni Subramaniam & Dr. Sipra Hota

Projects (kind of learning)

April 2025 — August 2025

Computational Study of Quasi-Normal Modes of Ellis–Bronnikov Wormholes

Supervisor: Dr. Suman Ghosh · Department of Physics, Birla Institute of Technology.

Undertook a supervised research training project focused on understanding and numerically reproducing the quasi-normal mode (QNM) analysis of Ellis–Bronnikov wormholes within a theoretical gravity framework. The work was based on an existing research study and aimed at developing familiarity with perturbation methods in curved spacetime.

Implemented standard computational techniques used in QNM analysis, including the WKB approximation, Prony method, and basic time-domain integration, to study their application in gravitational wave perturbation problems and black hole/wormhole spacetimes.

The project focused on learning and reproducing established numerical workflows, with emphasis on understanding the physical interpretation of quasi-normal ringing, numerical stability, and the behavior of perturbations in exotic compact object backgrounds.

Through this work, gained foundational exposure to gravitational wave physics, wormhole spacetimes, and numerical techniques used in general relativity, forming an early research base for further work in theoretical and computational astrophysics.
